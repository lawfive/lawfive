# Risks and technical notes

Known risks, platform limits, and what still needs checking on real hardware. IDs (R-n) are stable and never reused; R-1 to R-20 are taken.

## What still needs a real device

Most of LawFive has been checked in unit tests, the shared match cases, simulators and emulators. These are the checks that need real hardware. [The checklist for testing on a watch](../../watch-garmin/TESTING-ON-A-WATCH.md) covers the Garmin app.

- **R-6** **Garmin watches.** The app has refereed real matches on a Venu 2, syncing through the Android app: the vibrations, the screen staying on near a period's end, the activity and its GPS map in Garmin Connect, the track with heart rate and the compass kickoff direction all worked. Still to check on a watch:
  - **Reading it while running, and in sunlight:** each match screen layout (UI-21 to UI-25), the small time of day, the Ring's cost on AMOLED screens, the kits and the kickoff dot (UI-10, UI-11), the added time left and the time over it (TMR-16), the orange stopped clock (TMR-15), the sin bin pills (EVT-12), and the app icon in the app list on AMOLED and memory-in-pixel screens (UI-14).
  - **Alerts:** the added time alert told apart from full time's (VIB-7); one vibration at the length with stoppage time (VIB-1); the sin bin alert (EVT-12); the lightning countdown (TMR-13).
  - **Controls:** sideways team swipes (INP-10), the Back press only turning the screen on (INP-11), touch lock in rain (INP-12), touch and buttons with sweat and rain, card reasons on the narrowest round screen (EVT-14), the team menu's five rows (EVT-15), Adjust clock's entry and its minus sign in each model's fonts (TMR-14).
  - **Match flow:** the golden goal screen (ET-6), extra time's kickoff team and direction (ET-7), abandoning a match (EVT-11), a whole match of one period (SET-16), suspend and resume with the activity pausing in Garmin Connect (TMR-13), a goal with scorers off on a touch watch (EVT-10), an injury starting the stoppage (EVT-16), the clock-that-stops settings through Garmin Connect (TMR-15), moving an old screen layout to the default on an existing install (UI-24).
  - **Sync and data:** direct sync through Garmin Connect, typing a server address (SYNC-9), the distance at full time (SYNC-7), the forecast line (WX-7), storage over 90 minutes (TRK-7), a linked server kept on the watch, a sync during a server restart (R-19, SYNC-17), and a phone silencing the watch's notifications during a match (TIP-1); the planned match's short names from Garmin Connect's settings, with accented names (SET-5); a canceled match's red mark on the Venu 2's white menus, which is pale there (SYNC-16).
  - **Battery** over a full match with GPS (R-4).
- **R-12** **iPhone.** The app has run on simulators and on an iPad. Not yet checked on a real iPhone: the QR scanner and camera, notifications, background sync, the local network prompt, the app icon (light, dark and tinted) and launch screen, and the look in sunlight and at night (IOS-13). Garmin's iOS SDK can't start the app from a watch message as Android can, so the bridge (IOS-9) may need the app open at a ground without signal. Older iOS versions have drawn layouts differently from the simulators' newer ones, so a new layout is worth a look on the oldest supported iOS.
- **R-13** **Wear OS and Apple Watch.** Both have run mostly on an emulator and a simulator, and Wear OS on a real watch. Still to check on real watches:
  - The system's text entry (timer names, the server address; WCH-11, SYNC-9), which didn't hand text back on the Wear OS emulator.
  - Recording a real match (WCH-7): heart rate, GPS and speed, distance, battery, the workout with the screen off, and the copies in Apple Health and Health Connect. A short test match on a Galaxy Watch4 indoors, before the warm-up existed, recorded no GPS position and heart rate only after a minute.
  - A Galaxy Watch4's soccer exercise has no location, speed or distance, so the app takes the GPS straight from the watch: still to check there are GPS ready outdoors, a whole match's track with real speeds, the distance worked out from it against the server's, and battery with the GPS on through the breaks.
  - The warm-up before kickoff (ACT-5): GPS searching turning to ready with real satellites, the position held from warm-up into kickoff, a long wait with the screen off before kickoff, and how long the watch lets a warm-up run (Wear OS) or keeps a prepared workout session (Apple Watch); on Apple Watch, whether 25 m is the right point for GPS ready.
  - The compass (WCH-10), vibration strength, screen-off behavior, the physical buttons and the crown, the golden goal (ET-6), the kickoff direction picture (PRE-8), abandoning and the sync screen with a real connection (EVT-11, UI-26), sideways team swipes on Wear OS (INP-10), Mark pitch with real GPS (TRK-13), the Apple Watch's hardware identifier (STAT-15), linking the Apple Watch from the iPhone (WCH-12), Water Lock at each kickoff (INP-12), the alerts' intervals (Remind every, Repeat alerts every; VIB-5) felt during play, Sync on opening (WCH-13): a wrist raise mid-match not syncing, and a sync on coming back after a break between matches; the player number's Tap again (EVT-9) with wet fingers; and the server address check (SYNC-9) over LTE and through the phone.
  - Wear OS's foreground service type and the Apple Watch's workout session may need explaining to the stores' reviewers.
- **R-18** **Push notifications.** The relay at `push.lawfive.app` has delivered to an Android emulator, and to an iPhone simulator through APNs' sandbox: the iPhone's test notification, a weather risk and a new match from an integration, each written by its notification service extension from the server, a notice the server didn't have in the neutral words, and a weather risk's banner opening its match when tapped (IOS-14). On real devices, a Pixel 9 Pro (Android) and an iPad running the iPhone app from TestFlight (APNs' production side, signed as for the App Store with the App Group and Push Notifications), both locked and asleep, received the admin's test notification and a kickoff moved by an integration, the server counting it as reaching two phones; on the iPad the extension wrote it from the server while locked ("Kickoff moved: Test match", PitchLink Sync, now and was). Three matches added together more than a day ahead came as one notice with a line each, on both. Tapping a notification crashed build 202 on the iPad (the handler replied to iOS off the main thread); with the fix, build 203 opened the match list from a notice for several matches, and a venue change's Directions opened the map. Still to check: the daily limits in production, and on the iPhone, a canceled match's reminders going (its extension removes them). On the Pixel, a match canceled through an integration kept its four reminders after the notice: the notice's sync didn't plan the reminders again. Fixed in build 216, with the weather risk's sync too: on the Pixel with the app closed, a canceled match's four reminders went as its notice arrived. A match added and canceled within the same minute sends no notice (NOTE-2), so a phone that already synced the match keeps its reminders until its next sync. On a real iPhone: the extension writing in time on a slow connection.
- **R-20** **Plan limits from a phone offline.** Plans (PLAN-4) refuse a match, referee or venue a phone made offline once the account is at its limit; the phone keeps it and the sync fails for it, as for any refused save. Not yet tried on a phone. The watch's own recordings are never refused.

Android's checks still to do (APP-6, APP-13, APP-14, APP-27, APP-28): the scanner reading a real QR code, the camera opening Get the app, Open in the app, the launcher and notification icons, and the look in sunlight and at night; a reminder arriving on the minute while the phone is asleep, after a restart and after an update, a few minutes late with Alarms & reminders refused, and a day's matches in one notification in the shade. Deleting a match (RES-8) with and without signal on both phones.

## Platform limits

- **R-1 (Resolved)** On some touch watches a swipe right is the system Back gesture. The match screen opens the game menu on Back, so the app is never left by accident.
- **R-2** The Garmin app must be a full device app (not a widget or watch face) to record an activity.
- **R-3** Recording, GPS and the compass (PRE-8) need the `Fit`, `Positioning` and `Sensor` permissions in the Garmin manifest.
- **R-4** Battery: a full match with GPS (about 2 hours with half-time and added time) needs testing on the target watches.
- **R-5** If the app closes or crashes mid-match, the clocks resume correctly (TMR-8). The activity so far is saved, and recording continues as a new activity when the app reopens.
- **R-7** Touch watches deliver input twice (a tap as Select and then as a positioned tap; Start/Stop as a key and Select). All input on the match, number and confirmation screens goes through one handler that keeps them apart (INP-9).
- **R-8** Memory-in-pixel screens (for example non-AMOLED fēnix models) show only 64 colors, so team colors (UI-10) show as the nearest one, and the black or white text is chosen for the color as set.
- **R-9** A sideloaded Garmin build gets no phone settings (Garmin Connect offers settings only for apps from the Connect IQ Store), so the planned match, aliases and colors (SET-11) can't be set that way. Account sync covers them, and the server is chosen on the watch (SYNC-9) or built in at install (SYNC-8). A Store install (a private beta on a Venu 2) does get them: the app's Settings in Garmin Connect reach the watch (a home team name shows in New match), and clearing one goes back to the default.
- **R-11** Garmin's Android phone SDK and Garmin Connect deliver the watch's messages by broadcast. Garmin Connect never binds to the SDK's binding service, and registering for it stops the broadcasts, so the app doesn't use it: the SDK's receiver takes messages while the app runs, and a receiver in the manifest (`WatchMessageReceiver`) starts it otherwise. A force-stopped app gets no broadcasts until it's opened again, so the watch goes direct then. `adb logcat -s LawFiveWatch` shows what Connect IQ sees. The Android README covers the simulator's quirks.
- **R-16** The Apple Watch keeps a match running with a HealthKit workout session (WCH-5, WCH-7), which has no time limit. A whole match with the screen off on a real watch is still to check (R-13).
- **R-17** The iPhone app includes Garmin's Connect IQ SDK, a closed binary under Garmin's license with no license file or privacy manifest, so anyone building it accepts that license. App Store Connect raised nothing about it on upload; App Review still might. Until the bridge is proven on hardware, App Store builds leave it off: no `bluetooth-central` and no From this phone in Link a watch (IOS-9). The SDK stays in them, and so does the Bluetooth permission's reason, which the App Store asks for wherever Bluetooth code is.
- **R-19** The Garmin watch's requests straight through Garmin Connect (without the LawFive phone app) can't see an answer's headers, so APP-23 doesn't cover them; SYNC-17 goes by LawFive's JSON error instead, and checks `GET /v1/meta` when there isn't one. On a Venu 2 through Garmin Connect on Android, LawFive's errors arrive with their status and JSON, and a proxy's plain-text or HTML error (a 404, 502 or 503 while the server restarts) arrives as a failed request, which waits for the next sync. Still to check: a sync during a real restart, with a fee change, the match screen and pitch corners waiting, and Garmin Connect on an iPhone passing errors on the same way.

## Server

- **R-10** Photos (FILE-4) stored on disk live in the server's uploads volume; a deployment that doesn't mount one loses them when the container is replaced. Backups need the volume (or the bucket) as well as the database. LawFive doesn't encrypt photos in a bucket; that's the bucket's setting.
- **R-14** Resetting a forgotten password or changing the email address doesn't end the account's other sessions, so a stolen one keeps working until it's signed out (SEC-7) or expires. A watch's link is a session of its own kind, kept to syncing matches (SEC-13), so a reset could end every other session and leave the watches linked: [the accounts plan](../plans/accounts.md) proposes it.
- **R-15** Email addresses aren't confirmed, so anyone can sign up with an address that isn't theirs. One for an `ADMIN_EMAILS` address nobody has signed up with yet waits for an admin's approval, even when sign-up is open, and isn't made when it's closed (SEC-8), so whoever approves it needs to know it's that person's. An invitation makes its account without that wait, so one with no address set, used with an `ADMIN_EMAILS` address, makes an admin. Anyone who can read the server's logs can use the setup code (ADM-5) until the first account exists.
- Per-minute and per-day counts (PLAN-8) live in the server's memory, so a restart starts them again, and a second server process would count separately.
